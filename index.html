Periphery Passive Engagement

Historical video games can teach you even when you skip the cutscenes. Drawing on Lave and Wenger's legitimate peripheral participation, and on the work of Robert Rosenstone and Adam Chapman, I argue that players absorb historical knowledge through environmental design — architecture, landscape, color, and space — whether or not they engage with the narrative. Developers build these worlds alongside historians and archaeologists, so just moving through them teaches you something. Pick a scene below and click the hotspots: each one is something you'd pick up passively, without ever being told to study it.

How this works

The entire tool you just used is nothing more than a single data file of images plus short annotations, paired with a few dozen lines of display code. Every scene, hotspot, and caption lives in one editable file (data.js); the rest just reads that file and draws it on screen. A student in any field could fork this project, drop in their own images, rewrite the annotations, and have an interactive version of their own work — no frameworks, no build step, no server required.
